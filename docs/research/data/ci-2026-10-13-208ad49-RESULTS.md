**Conventions.** Speedup = median over rounds of time(baseline)/time(config) in the
same round. With 5 rounds a percentile bootstrap of that median equals the
minimum-maximum of the per-round ratios, so per-suite intervals are given as that
**range**; report.md labels some of them "95% CI", which are these ranges or
slightly narrower. Cross-suite medians carry a **two-level bootstrap** (suites
resampled, then rounds within each chosen suite; 10,000 resamples, fixed seed): it
captures which suites were chosen and the between-round noise within one job, not
the variation between runs or runners; for the tuning suites the three
independent runs show that directly. "Tests differing" = the most in any round
against that round's jsdom-default run, given as raw (without the job's flaky
tests: those whose status changes between the job's own jsdom-default runs,
warm-up included). **Version rule:** a suite enters cross-suite medians only if
every package resolves jsdom >= 20 (computed, "Version rule": from the versions
experiment's own-a meta records). Only **handsontable** fails it (core,
react-wrapper and vue3 on jsdom 16.7.0; angular-wrapper 20.0.3). It is reported
separately. The applications (Excalidraw jsdom 22.1.0, Wire 29.1.1 / jest-environment-jsdom
30.5.2, from dom-api-tracer corpus/apps.json, not a resolved-version record) pass
it. **Names:** the data's "learned isolation" (`warpdom-learned`,
`jsdom-learned`) is the paper's *measured isolation*.

- Forcing a newer jsdom onto an old Jest stops files from loading (handsontable 26
  and 30, dom-testing-library, downshift, react-router and part of React Spectrum
  under 30): "missing", not a behavior change.

  taiga-ui, as they should. handsontable on jsdom 20.0.3 (its core moved up from

- **jsdom alone vs the dependency tree:** a forced jsdom brings its own cssstyle,

  dependency. Under its own jsdom 25.0.1, cssstyle 4.1.0 → 4.6.0 changes **12 tests**

  entirely its CSS dependency's. Under jsdom 30.1.2, 12 of 29 are those same tests.
- Dependency checks (meta.json): naive-ui's own tree resolves cssstyle 5.2.1,
  outside jsdom 27.4.0's declared ^5.3.4 (own-a and own-b), and for jsdom 20.0.3 and
  26.1.0 cssstyle was forced to ^2.3.0 and ^4.2.1. No other variant needed forcing.
  None is NA besides taiga-ui 30.1.2.

## 9 Anomalies and things to watch

- **Budget stops:** baselines ariakit 1 timed round, CopilotKit 4, Excalidraw 2
  (`*.notes.txt`). Their intervals are ranges of 1-4 values, and the cross-suite
  medians are also given without ariakit.
- **NA:** Excalidraw warpdom-vm produced no results in every run (baselines-apps).
  handsontable on happy-dom has no timed round in any three-way run (runs 1-2: warm-up
  without time; run 3: warm-up 306.8 s, then the core package timed out every round,
  with no notes line because the warm-up had a time). taiga-ui jsdom 30.1.2 did not
  install (versions).
- **solid-primitives** is flagged "broken" in every jsdom round of the held-out
  three-way, A2/A3 and A4 jobs by the >5-test rule. That is an artifact of its 21
  misattributed pagination tests, not broken runs. Its slow order appears on warpdom
  only (Section 7). In baselines warpdom-default and warpdom-learned show one slow round
  (range down to 0.22x).
- **Runners:** all 4-core, 15.6 GiB, but six CPU models. Absolute times of a suite
  differ by up to 1.6-1.8x between runs (radix, analog, handsontable), while the
  per-round ratios are stable (spread at most 8.6% under the rule, handsontable 11.3%).
  All 14 runs ran concurrently (dispatched 16:00:31-16:02:29 UTC), so jobs of
  different experiments shared the account's concurrency, not machines.
- **Cross-suite bootstrap intervals** for measured isolation are wide (upper bounds
  2.6-4.9x) because of bimodal per-suite values. They are not evidence of large typical
  gains.
- **Reporting tools:** ablation-report.cjs prints no learning cost when the CLI
  records no t_sh (naive-ui, VueUse) and uses only learnings with both t_sh and
  t_split elsewhere (computed gives exact costs). report.md labels 5-round
  bootstrap intervals "95% CI" although they equal (or nearly equal) the range.
  `timing-report.cjs --help` is not implemented (it tries to read a file named
  `--help`; revision plan, R2 minor 1).
- `tools/classify-differences.cjs` crashed on a per-test map without tests (Excalidraw's
  warpdom-vm); fixed in the archive commit.
- The documentation gap: handsontable's 3 deliberate differences are not in
  PLAN.md's "Deliberate deviations" list.

## 10 Headline candidates

Supported, as stated:

1. **Drop-in speed with unchanged results.** Replacing jsdom with warpdom in each
   runner's default isolation was faster on all 13 tuning suites under the version
   rule in each of three independent runs: median **1.28x, 1.27x, 1.26x** (95%
   two-level bootstrap 1.22-1.54x, 1.21-1.54x, 1.19-1.55x), per suite 1.03x (solid;
   per-round range 0.97-1.06x) to 1.89x (MUI). Between-run spread of a suite's
   speedup: median 2.3%, max 8.6%. 9 of the 13 had no test differing from jsdom in
   any round; the rest are 7 ariakit tests that fail on jsdom, ark-ui tests that fail
   or are flaky on jsdom, one svelte deliberate deviation, and one unexplained solid
   test. Caveats: one runner class (4-core GitHub runners, six CPU models), a
   convenience corpus that warpdom was tuned on, and an upper interval bound that
   reflects suite choice.
2. **Held-out and applications.** On the 8 held-out suites with valid jsdom rounds,
   a median **1.36x (1.05-1.69x)** (all 9: 1.31x, 1.04-1.68x), 6 of 9 with no
   differing test. Caveat: the held-out set drove fixes after its first
   measurement, so it is not unseen any more. Applications: Excalidraw 1.21x
   (1.20-1.23x), Wire web app 1.03x (1.00-1.03x), 0 differing tests, a migration case
   study.

   first run after install**, 3 deliberate differences.
4. **Measured isolation over the best correct jsdom configuration:** a median
   **1.22x (1.02-1.25x)** on 11 tuning suites, 1.19x (1.02-1.33x) on 16 Vitest
   suites; 1.17x (0.98-1.26x) without the 1-round ariakit. Where no file leaks and

   Over the best correct jsdom configuration on those three: 1.62x, 1.17x, 1.49x.

   measured isolation beats the drop-in (2.46x over jsdom, 1.61x over the drop-in).
   Its results equal the drop-in's (no G2 violation found).

7. **Lazy interfaces and the query cache have no measurable whole-suite effect**

8. **WPT** (not an independent check: worked on during the study): 87.4% of subtests

**Not supported by these data:**

- That measured isolation is generally faster than the drop-in: the median is
  **1.00x**, and it gains on only 4 of 17 suites. That it is never slower: svelte 0.98x
  and VueUse 0.99x in every round (within about 2-3%).
- The earlier VueUse (8.89x) and Vuetify ("every jsdom alternative changes results,
  5.77x over the best") claims: VueUse now gains nothing from measured isolation,
  and Vuetify's cleanup baseline is correct at 4.87x.
- That measured isolation beats every correct jsdom setup: it is slower on ark-ui
  (0.55x), melt-ui (0.80x) and naive-ui (0.23x).
- "No unexplained differences": solid's transition test fails on warpdom only, with
  no demonstrated cause. melt-ui's Context Menu test is speed-sensitive by analogy
  only.
- That G2 holds: no violation was found, but the check has limited power
  (no shuffled orders; flaky passes are indistinguishable from leak-induced ones).
- The pre-registered 1.8x target: neither the drop-in (about 1.27x) nor measured
  isolation reaches it on the corpus median.
- That happy-dom is a drop-in alternative: similar median speed (1.25-1.28x on
  tuning), but it changes results on almost every suite.
- The WARPDOM_ABLATE-unset control at 8630317 (not run).
- Anything about other runner classes (more cores, other OSes) or Jest-level
  measured isolation (Jest always isolates).
